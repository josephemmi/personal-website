Da Mangiare — "to eat" — is where family recipes, kitchen experiments and food writing live. It draws heavily on a Venezuelan and Italian household, and on the belief that a recipe is never just a list of ingredients: it's a story about where it came from and who taught it to you.

The project started as an attempt to properly write down recipes that had only ever existed as half-remembered instructions from family members. It's grown into a broader practice of food writing — part recipe archive, part reflection on why food and identity are so tightly bound together.

## What it involves

- Documenting family recipes with proper measurements, for the first time in some cases
- Writing about food as it connects to place, memory and migration
- Ongoing experiments in the kitchen, some more successful than others

## Status

Actively growing, one dish at a time.
