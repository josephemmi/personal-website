## Context

Scottish Water serves almost every household and business in Scotland. For most of its history, customer contact meant a phone call — even for simple things like reporting a leak or checking a bill. Digital channels existed but were an afterthought, and call centre volumes were rising every year.

## Problem

Call demand was growing faster than the organisation could resource it, and a large proportion of calls were for things that didn't need a person: status updates, meter readings, simple account changes. Customers had no reliable way to self-serve, and the organisation had no clear model for what "good" self-service should look like for a utility that people rarely think about until something goes wrong.

## Discovery

We ran extensive research with customers across demographics and circumstances, including people who actively avoided digital channels. A recurring theme: people didn't want a slicker app, they wanted certainty — to know something had been received, was being dealt with, and roughly when it would be resolved. Trust, not convenience, was the real currency.

We also mapped the highest-volume call reasons against how "solvable" they were online, which gave us a prioritised, evidence-based backlog instead of a wish list.

## Product Decisions

- **Status over slickness.** Every self-service flow was designed around clear status and expectation-setting rather than visual polish alone.
- **Assisted digital, not digital-only.** We deliberately built flows that worked for people with low digital confidence, including plain-language content and generous error recovery.
- **Instrument everything.** We treated call deflection as a measurable product outcome from day one, not a retrospective claim.

## Delivery

I led product for the programme, working alongside content designers, researchers and engineering to ship a sequence of self-service journeys — leak reporting, account management, billing queries — each validated against real call center data before and after launch.

## Outcomes

- Meaningful, sustained reduction in call volume for the highest-frequency contact reasons.
- Measurable increase in customer-reported confidence in "knowing what happens next" after using self-service.
- A repeatable framework for prioritising future self-service investment based on call deflection potential, adopted beyond the original programme.

## Lessons Learned

In an essential service, self-service isn't a cost-cutting exercise — it's a trust exercise. The teams that treated it that way built things people actually used; the ones that treated it as a UI project didn't move the needle on calls at all.
