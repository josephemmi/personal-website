## Context

Storm ID is a Scottish digital product studio working across a range of sectors, from healthcare to financial services. Consulting there meant moving between client contexts frequently, each with its own constraints, stakeholders and level of digital maturity.

## Problem

Clients came in at very different starting points — some needed help defining what to build at all; others already had a roadmap but were struggling to deliver it. The common thread was a gap between ambition and the practical discipline needed to ship well.

## Discovery

Every engagement started with structured discovery: understanding the client's actual constraints (technical, organisational, commercial) rather than accepting the brief as originally framed. More than once, the real problem turned out to be different from the one we were initially asked to solve.

## Product Decisions

- Insist on a discovery phase even when clients wanted to skip straight to delivery.
- Right-size process to the client's context rather than applying a single consulting playbook everywhere.
- Prioritise transferring capability to client teams, not just shipping outputs.

## Delivery

Across engagements I worked as embedded product lead, running discovery, shaping roadmaps and working directly alongside client teams and Storm ID's design and engineering staff to deliver.

## Outcomes

- Delivered product strategy and early-stage builds across multiple client sectors.
- Built a reputation for reframing ambiguous briefs into scoped, deliverable problems.
- Several engagements led to longer-term relationships beyond the original scope of work.

## Lessons Learned

Consulting sharpens a specific skill that's easy to underrate: diagnosing quickly, without the luxury of months of context, what's actually going on inside an organisation. That skill has stayed useful in every product role since.
