Most of the friction between product teams and the rest of the business isn't about priorities — it's about a category error. Roadmaps get treated as plans, when they're better understood as a snapshot of current thinking, communicated to a specific audience, at a specific point in confidence.

A plan implies commitment. A roadmap, done honestly, implies "here's what we believe today, and here's how confident we are in each part of it." Those are very different documents, even when they look identical on a slide.

## Where it breaks down

The break happens when a roadmap is presented with plan-level certainty to manage stakeholder anxiety in the short term, and then quietly changes later without anyone acknowledging that the certainty was never real. Nobody said anything untrue in the moment, but trust erodes anyway, because the format implied more confidence than existed.

## A better default

State confidence explicitly, even when it's uncomfortable: this quarter is committed, next quarter is a strong intention, the quarter after is a direction we currently believe in. It's a harder conversation to have upfront, and a much easier one to have later, when reality inevitably disagrees with the plan.
