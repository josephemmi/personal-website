Every product organisation has a category of work that everyone agrees matters and almost nobody wants to own: the design system, the shared data model, the internal tooling that makes everything else faster. It doesn't ship features. It doesn't have a demo day. It's also, almost always, the difference between an organisation that gets faster over time and one that gets slower.

The reason it goes unowned isn't that people don't understand its value. It's that the value is diffuse and delayed, while the cost is immediate and concentrated on whoever picks it up. Nobody gets promoted for a design system that quietly prevented six months of accumulated inconsistency.

## Why it needs a name on it anyway

Infrastructure without an owner doesn't stay neutral — it decays. Every team makes a small, locally reasonable decision that adds friction somewhere else, and because nobody is accountable for the whole, nobody notices until the whole thing is unworkable.

Giving it an explicit owner, even part-time, changes the incentive. Someone now has to say no to a convenient shortcut, and someone now has standing to insist on consistency instead of hoping for it.

## The trade-off is real

This isn't free. Time spent maintaining shared foundations is time not spent on visible features. The organisations that get this right don't pretend that trade-off doesn't exist — they make it explicitly, on purpose, instead of letting it happen by default through neglect.
