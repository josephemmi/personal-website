Every recipe I grew up with was measured in the same units: a bit, enough, until it looks right. Nobody wrote anything down, because nobody needed to — you learned by watching, and the recipe lived in your hands rather than on paper.

Starting Da Mangiare meant confronting how much of that knowledge was at risk of just disappearing. So I started doing the annoying, slightly comic work of standing in the kitchen with a set of scales, asking someone to cook exactly as they always had, and translating "enough" into grams in real time.

## What gets lost in translation

Some things resist being written down. A recipe that says "cook until it smells right" is more honest, in its way, than one that says "12 minutes," because the real signal was never the clock. Converting it to a fixed measurement is useful for reproducibility, but it quietly discards the part of the knowledge that was about attention, not precision.

## Why I'm doing it anyway

Imperfect documentation beats no documentation. A recipe with slightly-too-precise measurements that someone can actually cook from in twenty years is worth more than a perfectly intuitive one that only exists in someone's memory. The goal isn't to capture everything — it's to make sure enough survives that someone else can pick it up.
