I've spent my life explaining where I'm from with a sentence instead of a word. Born in Venezuela, raised partly in Spain, settled now in Scotland — the short version is easy to say and doesn't explain very much.

The longer version is that moving between countries as a child teaches you a specific skill without meaning to: how to arrive somewhere with nothing familiar, figure out quickly what actually matters, and rebuild a sense of normal from scratch. You get good at reading a room you don't yet understand, because you have to.

## What it has to do with work

I didn't connect this to product work for a long time, but the pattern is the same one I use professionally: arrive in an unfamiliar context, figure out fast what's actually going on beneath the surface explanations, and start building from there instead of waiting until it feels fully familiar. Every new team, every new organisation, is a smaller version of the same exercise.

## What it has to do with everything else

It's also, I think, the root of the curiosity that shows up in the rest of this site — the food, the photography, the small daily-noticing habit of Attimino. When "normal" has been rebuilt a few times, you stop assuming things are fixed, and start paying more attention to how they got that way.
